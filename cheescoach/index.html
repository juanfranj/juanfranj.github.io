Why another chess app

Engines are free. Understanding isn't.

Any phone can run an engine stronger than any human alive. It shows you the move and a number. It never tells you what you walked into, and a number is not a lesson. CheesCoach keeps the engine and adds the part that was missing: a plain sentence about the idea you missed, written for your rating.

Practice Lab

A game has three parts. So does the training.

Not a pile of exercises. Three labs, one for each phase of the game, each with the same shape: learn the idea first, then play it out against an opponent that will punish you for getting it wrong.

The three labs are Premium. The Italian Game in the opening trainer, and the first middlegame exercise, are free.

I — The opening

Your repertoire, until it sticks

Pick the openings you actually play. The trainer drills the moves with spaced repetition and tells you when a line is due for review. Then you play the same line out against Maia, a network trained on real club games, so it goes wrong the way humans go wrong.

How the coach is powered

Bring your own key, or don't think about it

The engine runs on your phone. The explanations come from a language model, and you choose how you pay for that: credits inside the app, or your own API key with one of five providers, billed by them at cost.

Credits

Buy a pack and forget about it. Free calls to start with, so you can see what the coach sounds like before paying anything.

Your key

Paste an API key and the app talks to that provider directly. Typical cost of a full game review is a fraction of a cent.

Privacy

Keys are encrypted on the device and never leave it except to reach the provider you chose. No ads, no trackers, no selling anybody's games.
